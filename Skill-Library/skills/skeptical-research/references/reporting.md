# Audit trail and application

Scale the output to the decision. A concise answer may carry its audit trail in linked notes;
do not force every investigation into a large report or persist files without a reason.

Lead with the conclusion and applicable conditions. Then provide the decisive evidence and
reasoning, credible alternatives, verification results, uncertainty, and next action.

## Evidence and claim records

For sources supporting decisive claims, record:

- author/institution, title, direct URL, publication/update date, access date, version/edition;
- precise section, page, paragraph, table, figure, or code location;
- what was actually accessible and read; unknown metadata stays unknown;
- the supported claim, evidential role, methods and limitations, and shared-source lineage.

Link sources near the supported claims. Primary evidence and a disciplinary synthesis serve
different purposes. Do not cite an inaccessible full text as if inspected or a method guide as
proof of an empirical result. Avoid broad claims supported only by narrow examples.

Label consequential statements as facts/observations, hypotheses, deductions, interpretations,
or preferences. Label pivotal premises verified, assumed, or inferred. Explain confidence through
the strength and scope of evidence; numerical confidence requires a justified method and inputs.

## Verification status

| Status | Required disclosure |
| --- | --- |
| Locally executed | Procedure/command, inputs and their origin, environment, outputs, criterion, limitations |
| Published by third parties | Who performed it, source location, methods and conditions inspected, transfer limits |
| Documentary analysis | Documents/passages inspected, comparison criteria, observed wording versus interpretation |
| Proposed / not executed | Protocol, acceptance criteria, missing resource or authorization, no claimed outcome |

Separate correctness within a model from fit to real conditions. Record contradictions, affected
premises, revisions, and dependent checks repeated. A proposed pilot is not successful validation.

## Decision and stopping record

Report the research budget, actual coverage, and why work stopped: sufficient decision support,
budget reached, or a specific missing resource. Do not claim exhaustive coverage from repeated
searches. Name the unresolved dependency most likely to change the conclusion.

Choose a supported disposition:

- **Apply within stated conditions:** evidence supports this scope; disclose material residual risk.
- **Pilot before broader use:** state representative conditions, acceptance and stop criteria,
  monitoring, and reversibility; mark the pilot proposed unless it ran.
- **Remain provisional / do not apply yet:** identify the decisive gap and cheapest informative check.
- **Interpretive conclusion:** state the strongest defensible reading and unresolved rivals,
  including limits on using it as a factual, causal, or normative claim.

Compare alternatives with the same criteria, including the baseline and a simpler option where
plausible. An inconclusive result is useful when it identifies what observation would decide.
