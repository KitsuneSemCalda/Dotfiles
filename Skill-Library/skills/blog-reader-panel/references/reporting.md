# Reporting the review

Adapt the length to the material. For a short draft, compress sections; for a sampled blog, retain
the structure and identify the sampled posts.

## Reading boundary

State what was reviewed, whether it was a cold read, the likely audience inferred from the work,
and any important access or sampling limitation.

## Outside view

Open with a concise account of what the work appears to be trying to do, what impression it creates,
and what a reader is most likely to remember. Treat inferred intent as an inference.

## Panel reactions

For each selected persona, give:

- its relationship to the material;
- initial response and expectation;
- what works for that reader;
- friction, doubt, or loss of attention;
- likely takeaway or unanswered question.

Keep the voices distinct but concise. Support consequential reactions with concrete passages or
patterns rather than invented quotations from the persona.

## Three-layer assessment

Assess content, writing, and idea separately. For each layer, identify its strongest quality and
most important limitation. Explain interactions between layers when relevant—for example, a sound
idea obscured by structure or confident prose outrunning the evidence.

## Synthesis

Show:

- panel consensus;
- meaningful disagreement or audience tradeoffs;
- the gap, if any, between apparent intention and likely reception;
- recurring tendencies across posts when more than one was reviewed.

## Revision priorities

Recommend a short ordered set of changes. Each recommendation should name the observed problem,
the reader effect, the proposed intervention, and what should be preserved. Separate high-impact
revisions from optional taste-level refinements.

End with two compact statements:

- **Protect this:** the quality most essential to the work's identity.
- **Test next:** the most valuable unresolved question, reader test, or revision experiment.
