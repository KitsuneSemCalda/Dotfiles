# Persona selection

Use personas to model different reading conditions, incentives, knowledge, and standards of proof.
Choose the smallest panel that creates meaningful contrast, usually three to five lenses.

## Core lenses

### Intended reader

Infer a plausible target reader from the subject, vocabulary, framing, and publication context.
Assess whether the text delivers the value it appears to promise and whether it respects this
reader's time and knowledge.

### Intelligent skeptic

Engage seriously but test the central claim, causal reasoning, evidence, unstated assumptions, and
alternative explanations. Skepticism is not hostility or reflexive disagreement.

### Context-poor newcomer

Read without the author's history, specialist vocabulary, or implicit references. Find the earliest
point where missing context changes comprehension, confidence, or motivation to continue.

## Optional lenses

Select only lenses relevant to the material:

- **Domain peer:** tests technical or subject-matter accuracy, nuance, and contribution beyond what
  informed readers already know.
- **Sympathetic dissenter:** shares the concern or values but disputes the framing, proposed cause,
  or conclusion.
- **Time-poor subscriber:** tests the opening, structure, information density, and memorable value.
- **Literary editor:** examines voice, rhythm, imagery, structure, and the relationship between form
  and meaning.
- **Potential sharer:** asks whether the work can be accurately described, quoted, or recommended
  and what reputational hesitation might prevent sharing.
- **Future reader:** tests whether references, claims, and context will remain intelligible outside
  the publication moment.

Create a custom lens when the subject has a clear stakeholder omitted above. Define it by its
relationship to the text, relevant knowledge, motivation, and standard of proof—not by decorative
personal details.

## Guardrails

- Do not simulate demographic groups as monoliths.
- Do not infer sensitive traits about the author or audience.
- Do not use personas to manufacture false consensus or predictable praise/criticism.
- Distinguish a lens-specific preference from a broadly consequential problem.
- If lived experience is central and unavailable, identify the missing perspective instead of
  claiming authentic representation.
