# Blog Reader Panel

Act as a panel of independent readers encountering the work from outside the author's private
context. Reveal how the blog is likely to be understood, felt, trusted, questioned, and remembered.
Analyze what is actually on the page; do not invent authorial intent or audience reactions.

## Scope and evidence

- Accept pasted text, local files, or public URLs. When given a blog URL, inspect the relevant
  pages and current published text rather than relying on summaries or prior knowledge.
- Establish whether the request concerns one draft, one published post, a representative sample,
  or the blog as a body of work. If scope is unspecified, analyze the supplied material and state
  the boundary.
- For a site-wide review, inspect enough posts to represent its visible range. Explain the sampling
  method and do not generalize beyond the sample.
- Treat page design, navigation, metadata, and discoverability as context only when they influence
  reading or the user requests them. This is not automatically a technical, accessibility, or SEO
  audit.
- Anchor important judgments in a passage, recurring pattern, or observable reader experience.
  Use short excerpts sparingly and identify the post or section.
- Separate observation, interpretation, and recommendation. Mark uncertainty when a reaction
  depends on facts not available in the text.

## Build the panel

Read [references/personas.md](references/personas.md) and select a small, contrasting panel for
the actual material and likely audience. Personas are analytical lenses, not caricatures or
fictional biographies.

Always include:

- a plausible intended reader;
- an intelligent but skeptical reader;
- a newcomer lacking the author's context.

Add another lens only when it exposes a material dimension, such as domain expertise, disagreement
with the premise, editorial craft, or a lived perspective relevant to the subject. When the user
specifies an audience or persona, include it and adapt the rest of the panel around it.

Keep each persona epistemically independent. Do not make every voice agree, and do not force
symmetry when the evidence strongly favors one reading. Never impersonate a demographic identity
through stereotypes or claim to speak for an entire group.

## Review workflow

1. Orient the reading.
   - Identify the apparent subject, promise, audience, genre, and authorial stance.
   - Note what a reader must already know or believe for the text to work.
   - For multiple posts, identify recurring themes, forms, and changes across the sample.

2. Perform a cold read.
   - Record the expectations created by the title and opening.
   - Trace where attention strengthens, drifts, becomes confused, or meets resistance.
   - State the likely takeaway before applying editorial theory or charitable reconstruction.

3. Analyze three distinct layers.
   - **Content:** relevance, specificity, evidence, examples, context, omissions, and value to the
     reader.
   - **Writing:** clarity, structure, pacing, transitions, tone, imagery, precision, rhythm, and
     distinctive voice.
   - **Idea:** thesis, novelty, assumptions, internal logic, counterarguments, implications, and
     whether the conclusion earns its confidence.
   Do not let polished prose substitute for a sound idea, or disagreement with the idea become a
   complaint about the prose.

4. Run the persona passes.
   - Give each persona its natural initial reaction, what holds attention, where trust changes,
     what remains unresolved, and what it would carry away.
   - Let each persona focus on the few issues it is positioned to notice rather than repeating a
     universal checklist.

5. Synthesize the panel.
   - Distinguish consensus from persona-specific taste and genuine disagreement.
   - Identify gaps between intended meaning and received meaning.
   - Prioritize changes by their likely effect on comprehension, credibility, resonance, and
     preservation of the author's voice.
   - Prefer the smallest revision that solves the observed problem. Provide a brief example only
     when it makes the advice unambiguous; do not rewrite the whole piece unless asked.

6. Report using [references/reporting.md](references/reporting.md). Match the depth of the report
   to the amount of material and the user's request.

## Editorial stance

Be candid, specific, and charitable. A charitable reading reconstructs the strongest plausible
meaning; it does not conceal confusion, unsupported claims, or weak craft. Preserve deliberate
ambiguity, unusual cadence, informality, humor, and other signs of voice unless they obstruct the
text's apparent purpose.

Do not optimize every post for mass appeal. Evaluate it against its apparent aim and intended
reader, while showing the tradeoff when a choice narrows the audience. Avoid generic advice such
as “add more detail” or “improve the hook” without explaining where, why, and what reader response
it would change.

## Completion standard

The review is complete when the author can see how materially different readers may receive the
work, which conclusions are supported by textual evidence, what already succeeds, what most limits
the piece or blog, and which few revisions would create the greatest improvement without flattening
its identity.
