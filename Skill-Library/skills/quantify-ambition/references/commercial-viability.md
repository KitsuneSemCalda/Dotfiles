# Commercial viability

Use this reference only when generating income is part of the project's purpose. Model the
actual business shape rather than applying SaaS metrics to every venture.

## Start with the economic actor

Identify separately:

- the user who experiences the product;
- the customer who decides to buy;
- the payer who transfers money;
- the beneficiary who receives the outcome;
- any supplier, creator, worker, or partner required to deliver it.

These may be the same person, but do not assume so. Define the costly problem, current
alternative, buying trigger, decision process, switching cost, willingness to pay, and reason
this project could win. A large audience without a credible buyer is not a market.

## Market size

Prefer a bottom-up model:

```text
reachable customers
× realistic annual revenue per customer
= serviceable obtainable revenue
```

Build outward from a specific initial segment and distribution capacity. Use top-down market
reports only as context or a reasonableness check. Distinguish:

- theoretical demand;
- serviceable demand under the product and geographic constraints;
- reachable demand through identified channels;
- plausible captured demand within the modeled time horizon.

State the date, geography, segment definition, and source for external market facts. Do not
present TAM as a revenue forecast.

## Revenue model

Choose metrics that match how money is earned.

### Subscription or SaaS

- monthly and annual recurring revenue (MRR/ARR);
- logo and revenue churn, retention, and net revenue retention;
- average revenue per account and expansion or contraction;
- trial or signup activation, paid conversion, and time to value;
- customer acquisition cost (CAC), gross margin, lifetime value assumptions, and CAC payback;
- committed recurring costs and cash runway.

Do not use `LTV = ARPU / churn` without stating its strong assumptions. Prefer cohort evidence
once available, and keep early LTV provisional.

### Transactional product or commerce

- orders or transactions, frequency, average order value, take rate or contribution margin;
- returns, refunds, fraud, payment costs, fulfillment, inventory turns, and working capital;
- repeat purchase and cohort contribution after variable costs.

### Marketplace

- active supply and demand on the same geography, category, and time slice;
- match or fill rate, time to match, transaction frequency, gross merchandise value, and take
  rate;
- supply and demand retention, leakage outside the platform, trust and safety, and subsidies;
- liquidity thresholds rather than total registrations.

### Services or consulting

- qualified pipeline, close rate, average contract value, utilization, delivery capacity,
  gross margin, collection time, and concentration risk;
- founder or specialist hours required per unit of revenue;
- productization or hiring thresholds if growth depends on reducing bespoke work.

### Media, audience, or creator business

- qualified reach, engaged audience, retention, direct relationship ownership, and conversion
  by offer;
- revenue concentration across advertising, sponsorship, membership, products, licensing, or
  services;
- production capacity and dependence on a platform's algorithm or policies.

### Licensing, open-core, or developer product

- active implementations, production adoption, organizations, maintainers, and time to first
  value;
- conversion from free use to support, hosted service, enterprise features, or licensing;
- support burden, ecosystem health, cloud-provider risk, and free/paid boundary stability.

For a hybrid business, model each revenue stream separately before combining them.

## Funnel and growth model

Describe the path using counts and rates with a common time window:

```text
qualified prospects
× acquisition response
× activation
× paid conversion
× retention
× revenue per retained customer
= recurring or repeatable revenue
```

Adapt the steps to the actual buying process. For enterprise sales, include sales-cycle length,
procurement, pilots, and implementation capacity. For self-serve products, include discovery,
activation, time to value, and cohort retention.

Separate channel capacity from conversion. A high conversion rate on a tiny channel does not
support an ambitious scale. Identify saturation, competition, seasonality, and dependence on
paid platforms or personal reach.

## Unit economics and cash

At each relevant scale, estimate:

- price and realized revenue after discounts, taxes, refunds, and payment loss;
- variable cost to acquire, onboard, deliver, support, and retain;
- contribution margin per customer, order, transaction, or engagement;
- fixed operating costs and step changes in staffing or infrastructure;
- cash timing, runway, working capital, and financing needs;
- founder compensation and unpaid labor when they materially affect sustainability.

Show the break-even equation and the number of active customers, transactions, billable hours,
or units required. Do not label a business profitable while excluding recurring labor needed
to operate it.

## Pre-revenue evidence

Before reliable revenue exists, rank evidence by commitment rather than attention:

1. Paid use or signed contract.
2. Deposit, preorder, paid pilot, or binding commitment.
3. Repeated use of a working solution with a defined buyer.
4. Customer effort such as integration, migration, or procurement.
5. Qualified waitlist or explicit introduction to the decision-maker.
6. Interview statements, survey intent, clicks, likes, or broad signups.

Weak evidence can guide discovery but must not be presented as validated willingness to pay.

## Commercial metric tree

Connect four layers:

1. **Customer outcome:** measurable value the customer receives.
2. **Behavior:** use or workflow showing that value is occurring.
3. **Commercial result:** retention, repeat purchase, expansion, margin, or another durable
   economic result.
4. **Sustainability guardrails:** support burden, quality, concentration, cash, ethics, and
   founder or team health.

A revenue target without the customer outcome and delivery economics is incomplete.

## Decision thresholds

Define evidence that would justify:

- investing further in product or distribution;
- changing segment, positioning, price, or channel;
- replacing a high-touch process with automation;
- hiring or committing fixed costs;
- seeking external capital versus growing from revenue;
- pausing or abandoning the commercial thesis.

Targets must be labeled as strategic choices. Benchmarks can inform them but cannot choose
them for the user.
