# Quantified thesis format

Adapt the depth to the maturity of the idea. An early vision may need broad ranges and a short
measurement plan; a mature proposal may justify a detailed model.

```markdown
# Quantified Ambition: <project>

## Vision and intended change
<What should become different, for whom, and why the project is ambitious.>

## Ambition in concrete terms
<The handful of quantities that define the full vision.>

## Assumptions and evidence
| Claim or input | Value/range | Class | Evidence or rationale | Confidence |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | Known / Benchmark / Estimate / Target / Limit / Unknown | ... | ... |

## Scale scenarios
| Dimension | Conservative | Base | Ambitious |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Reach or impact | ... | ... | ... |
| Technical capacity | ... | ... | ... |
| People/time/resources | ... | ... | ... |
| Sustainability | ... | ... | ... |

## Metric tree
### Outcome
<The real-world result and how it is measured.>

### Leading indicators
<Signals available before the final outcome.>

### Guardrails
<What must not degrade while pursuing scale or speed.>

## Commercial thesis
<Include only for income-generating projects. Identify the buyer, painful problem, current
alternative, market model, revenue mechanism, funnel, unit economics, break-even point, cash
needs, and commercial decision thresholds. Keep each important number classified as known,
benchmark, estimate, target, limit, or unknown.>

## Staged proofs
| Stage | Question | Evidence required | Resource envelope | Exit or revision criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |

## Dominant constraints and risks
<The variables, thresholds, or assumptions most likely to determine the result.>

## Decision rules
- Accelerate when ...
- Redesign when ...
- Reduce scope when ...
- Pause or stop when ...

## Highest-value next evidence
<The smallest research, measurement, prototype, or test that most reduces uncertainty.>

## Open numbers
<Material values that cannot yet be estimated responsibly and how to obtain them.>
```

## Writing standard

- Lead with conclusions, not a wall of calculations.
- Keep units visible and use consistent time windows.
- Put formulas near the estimates they produce.
- Cite current external facts near the relevant claim.
- Explain why each central number matters to a decision.
- Preserve disagreement between scenarios instead of averaging it away.
- Call out when the vision contains mutually incompatible targets.

Do not end with a generic list of KPIs. End with the evidence that should be obtained next and
the decision it will inform.
